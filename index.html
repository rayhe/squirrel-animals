The Squirrel Squad

Squirrels are not just the fluffy-tailed acrobats in the park. They are a huge family of more than 280 cousins, including chipmunks, prairie dogs, marmots, and even squirrels that glide through the night sky.

Eastern Gray Squirrel

The Park Acrobat

Size
14 to 21 inches long with tail; 3/4 to 1 1/2 pounds
Home
Hardwood forests, city parks, and backyards across eastern North America
Menu
Acorns, hickory nuts, mushrooms, fruits, and buds

Superpower: Buries thousands of nuts each fall and forgets some of them. The forgotten nuts grow into new trees, so gray squirrels plant forests by accident.

Fun fact: An adult can munch up to 3 ounces of shelled nuts in a single day.

Fox Squirrel

The Big Orange

Size
19 to 29 inches long with tail; 1 to 3 pounds. The biggest tree squirrel in North America!
Home
Open oak and hickory woods
Menu
Acorns, hickory nuts, walnuts, and corn

Superpower: On cold mornings it sleeps in instead of getting up early. Definitely not a morning squirrel.

Fun fact: You can tell it from a gray squirrel by its orange belly and square-shaped face.

American Red Squirrel

The Cone Hoarder

Size
11 to 14 inches long with tail; 7 to 9 ounces
Home
Spruce and pine forests across the northern US and Canada
Menu
Seeds from conifer cones, berries, buds, and mushrooms

Superpower: Builds cone stashes that can grow 15 to 18 feet wide, and hangs mushrooms on tree branches to dry, like a tiny pantry.

Fun fact: It defends its territory all year with loud chattering calls. Some people call it the chickaree.

Eastern Chipmunk

The Cheek Champion

Size
8 to 11 inches long with tail; 2.3 to 4.5 ounces
Home
Forests, parks, and backyards in eastern North America; lives in underground burrows
Menu
Nuts, seeds, berries, mushrooms, and insects

Superpower: Can stuff up to 9 nuts into its cheek pouches at once: 4 in each cheek and one held in its teeth.

Fun fact: It does not truly hibernate. It naps through winter and wakes up for snacks from its burrow pantry. One chipmunk can gather up to 165 acorns in a single day.

Black-tailed Prairie Dog

The Town Crier

Size
14 to 17 inches long with tail; 1.5 to 3 pounds
Home
Grasslands of the Great Plains; lives in huge underground towns
Menu
Grasses, seeds, roots, and flowering plants

Superpower: Its towns can hold up to a million prairie dogs. Family groups called coteries greet each other by touching teeth, which looks like a kiss.

Fun fact: It has different alarm barks for different predators, one for hawks and another for coyotes. Burrowing owls move into empty prairie dog tunnels.

Groundhog

The Deep Sleeper

Size
16 to 27 inches long; 4 to 14 pounds
Home
Fields, meadows, and woodland edges across North America
Menu
Leaves, clover, flowers, grasses, and garden veggies

Superpower: A true hibernator that sleeps from October to February. Its burrows can be 8 to 66 feet long with separate rooms, including bathrooms.

Fun fact: Groundhog Day is February 2. Legend says if the groundhog sees its shadow, winter lasts six more weeks. Also called the woodchuck, it is one of 14 marmot species.

Northern Flying Squirrel

The Night Glider

Size
10 to 12 inches long with tail; 4 to 6.5 ounces
Home
Conifer forests across Canada and the northern US; nests in tree holes
Menu
Seeds, nuts, lichens, mushrooms, and truffles

Superpower: It does not fly. It glides on furry skin flaps stretched from wrist to ankle, soaring up to 150 feet and steering with its flat tail.

Fun fact: Its big eyes help it see at night. It spreads mushroom spores through the forest, which helps new trees grow.

California Ground Squirrel

The Snake Whisperer

Size
About 15 to 19 inches long with tail; 1 to 2 pounds
Home
Grasslands and oak woodlands in California and Oregon; lives in burrow colonies
Menu
Seeds, nuts, grains, roots, and insects

Superpower: When it spots a rattlesnake, it waves its tail and pumps extra heat into it, sending a clear signal: I see you, do not bother.

Fun fact: It is called a ground squirrel, but it is a true member of the squirrel family, just like the ones in the trees.
